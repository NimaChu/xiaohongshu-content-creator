## 1. Freshness rules

Search before writing when the topic includes:

- current models, product features, pricing, availability, or company roles;
- newly introduced terminology;
- laws, policies, standards, security, health, or financial advice;
- named experts and attributed viewpoints;
- precise benchmarks, dates, or adoption numbers.

Stable conceptual explanations may rely on general knowledge, but verify named product behavior used as an example.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. official documentation, release notes, repositories, and product pages;
2. original papers, talks, interviews, blog posts, or social posts from the named expert;
3. credible journalism or technical analysis;
4. community discussion only as a lead, not final evidence.

## 3. Claim table

Use this format in `research.md`:

| Claim | Source | Date | Confidence | Use? | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | High/Medium/Low | Yes/No | fact, analogy, or inference |

## 4. Attributed viewpoints

- Quote only when the exact wording is available.
- Keep quotations short.
- Otherwise paraphrase and label the text as a paraphrase.
- Do not fabricate an expert opinion to strengthen generic advice.
- Distinguish the source's claim from the creator's analogy.

## 5. Technical examples

Make commands, code, and workflows executable or label them as schematic. Check operating-system differences. Avoid destructive commands in beginner content unless the risk is explicitly explained.

## 6. Citation handoff

Keep images visually light. Put detailed citations in `post.md`, `research.md`, or a source appendix. Cite time-sensitive claims in the final response.
